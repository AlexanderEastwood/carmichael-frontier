\begin{abstract}
Let $\Smin$ denote the smallest Carmichael number with exactly $64$ distinct prime factors
(the $k=64$ entry of OEIS \href{https://oeis.org/A006931}{A006931}, currently unknown). We give
an explicit two--sided bound. For the upper bound we exhibit a $148$--digit Carmichael number
$N$ with exactly $64$ prime factors, so $\Smin \le N$. For the lower bound we prove, by a finite
exhaustion over a certified prime universe, that no $64$--factor Carmichael number is smaller than
$10^{145}$. Together,
\[
  10^{145} \;\le\; \Smin \;\le\; N \;<\; 10^{148},
\]
so $\Smin$ has $146$, $147$, or $148$ decimal digits. All arithmetic is exact. The lower bound is
a reproducible computation: the admissible universe is bounded by a Korselt cofactor argument, the
resulting bound is recomputed independently, and the exhaustion is carried out by three separate
enumeration implementations, one of which takes its Korselt verdict at every leaf from a frozen
reference oracle. The same cofactor minimum transfers to the next factor count, giving
$10^{148}\le S_{65}\le U_{65}$ for an explicit verified $151$--digit Carmichael number $U_{65}$, so
the smallest $65$--factor Carmichael number has $149$, $150$, or $151$ digits (\S\ref{sec:s65}). The author is responsible for the mathematics and code; see the acknowledgements
for the role of AI assistance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement}

A \emph{Carmichael number} is a composite integer $n$ with $b^{n}\equiv b \pmod n$ for all integers
$b$. By Korselt's criterion, a composite integer $n$ is Carmichael if and only if it is squarefree
and $(p-1)\mid(n-1)$ for every prime $p\mid n$. Write $\omega(n)$ for the number of distinct prime
factors. The sequence $S_k=\min\{\,n : n \text{ Carmichael},\ \omega(n)=k\,\}$ is
OEIS~A006931; values are known for $3\le k\le 63$ (R.~G.~E.~Pinch for the smaller $k$; the
$k=36$--$63$ values from the Butler University computation, collected in J.~Webster's repository
cited below), and $S_{64}$ is open as of September~2026. This note proves:

\begin{theorem}[Interval]\label{thm:interval}
$10^{145}\le \Smin \le N$, where
\[
  N \;=\; 8664205557705627135892897321176984054850894080429746661350141011836598584916617361518185311595941924456408326192593430956147737016566872025613568001
\]
is a $148$--digit Carmichael number with exactly $64$ prime factors. Consequently $\Smin$ has
$146$, $147$, or $148$ decimal digits, and the remaining exclusion interval for $\Smin$ is
$[10^{145},N)$.
\end{theorem}

The two bounds are proved in \S\ref{sec:upper} and \S\ref{sec:lower}. We use throughout the
following elementary consequence of Korselt's criterion.

\begin{lemma}[Pairwise admissibility]\label{lem:adm}
If $p<q$ are distinct primes dividing a Carmichael number $n$, then $p\nmid(q-1)$. Moreover $n$ is
odd.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $q\mid n$ we have $(q-1)\mid(n-1)$, so $n\equiv 1\pmod{d}$ for every divisor $d$ of $q-1$. If
$p\mid(q-1)$ then $n\equiv 1\pmod p$; but $p\mid n$ gives $n\equiv 0\pmod p$, forcing $p\mid 1$, a
contradiction. Oddness: no even integer $>2$ is Carmichael, since for an odd prime factor $p$ the
even number $p-1$ cannot divide the (then even) $n-1$ consistently with squarefreeness; concretely
$2$ cannot be one of $k\ge 3$ distinct prime factors of a Carmichael number.
\end{proof}

\section{Upper bound}\label{sec:upper}

The number $N$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:interval} is the product of the $64$ primes
\[
\begin{aligned}
&19,\,29,\,31,\,37,\,41,\,43,\,47,\,53,\,61,\,67,\,71,\,73,\,79,\,89,\,97,\,101,\,103,\,109,\,113,\,127,\\
&131,\,137,\,139,\,151,\,157,\,163,\,167,\,181,\,193,\,197,\,199,\,211,\,239,\,241,\,251,\,257,\,271,\,277,\,281,\,307,\\
&313,\,331,\,337,\,353,\,379,\,397,\,401,\,421,\,433,\,449,\,461,\,463,\,491,\,541,\,547,\,577,\,599,\,631,\,673,\,811,\\
&829,\,883,\,1951,\,3697.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are $64$ distinct primes; re-derive $N$ by multiplying the list rather than trusting the
printed decimal. Each factor is at most $3697$, so its primality is certified by exact trial
division (a deterministic check at this size). One verifies directly that $(p-1)\mid(N-1)$ for every
$p$; equivalently, writing $\lambda(N)=\operatorname{lcm}_{p\mid N}(p-1)=589416059232000$, one has
$N\equiv 1\pmod{\lambda(N)}$. Hence $N$ is a squarefree Carmichael number with $\omega(N)=64$, and
$\Smin\le N$. Since $10^{147}\le N<10^{148}$, the number $N$ has $148$ digits. The certificate is
verified by \code{ref/ref\_carmichael.py::verify\_certificate}, an independent, dependency--free
routine (squarefreeness, distinctness, per--factor primality, and Korselt) sharing no code with the
search that produced $N$.

\begin{remark}
$N$ was found by an exchange meet--in--the--middle search at modulus $M=3\,\lambda(N)=1768248177696000$
(so $N\equiv 1\pmod M$; $M$ is the search modulus, \emph{not} $\lambda(N)$ itself). It is an
incumbent, not claimed minimal, and improves a previously reported $149$--digit candidate. Its
$148$ digits are consistent with the known neighbours only in the following precise sense: Webster's
$N_{63}$ has $145$ digits, hence $N_{63}<10^{145}\le\Smin$, and the $N_{65}$ candidate has $151$
digits, hence exceeds $N$. This uses the proved interval and the stated digit lengths; it is not an
appeal to any general monotonicity of $S_k$.
\end{remark}

\section{Lower bound}\label{sec:lower}

Fix $Y=10^{145}$. We show no $64$--factor Carmichael number lies below $Y$.

\subsection{A certified prime universe}

\begin{lemma}[Cofactor lower bound]\label{lem:C}
There is an explicit constant
\[
  C \;=\; 1812140595092971804464959059612576188399713525008997620073689836850955613086867158799858934921557347504370550240044086773665823716701403213549
\]
(a $142$--digit integer) such that for every Carmichael number $n$ with $\omega(n)=64$ and every
prime factor $q\mid n$, the product of the remaining $63$ factors satisfies $n/q\ge C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof (finite computation)]
Fix such an $n$ and a prime factor $q$; let $p_1<\dots<p_{63}$ be the other factors, all odd
(Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}) and pairwise admissible. Let $S$ be the set of these that are $\le 97$; there
are finitely many internally admissible such $S$. For a fixed $S$, let $b_1(S)<b_2(S)<\cdots$ be the
primes $>97$ that are \emph{individually} admissible with every element of $S$ (i.e.\ $p\nmid(b-1)$
for all $p\in S$). The actual cofactor tail $r_1<\dots<r_{63-|S|}$ of $n$ consists of primes $>97$
each admissible with $S$, so $r_i\ge b_i(S)$ for every $i$. Hence
\[
  n/q \;=\; \prod_{p\in S}p \cdot \prod_{i=1}^{63-|S|} r_i
  \;\ge\; F(S) \;:=\; \Big(\prod_{p\in S}p\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{63-|S|} b_i(S)
  \;\ge\; \min_{S} F(S) \;=\; C.
\]
Three points make this rigorous: (i) the $b_i(S)$ need not be pairwise admissible with one another,
but ignoring those further constraints only decreases the product, so $F(S)$ is a genuine lower
bound; (ii) the computation uses a finite prime table (odd primes $\le 10^4$) and checks, for every
enumerated $S$, that it contains enough eligible tail primes, so no smaller completion is truncated
away; (iii) the cofactor $n/q$ itself need not be Carmichael. The minimum is taken over all
$407760$ admissible $S$. Thus $C$ is a certified lower bound on the cofactor product; it is not
claimed to be the smallest possible admissible $63$--factor product.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Universe]\label{cor:univ}
Every $64$--factor Carmichael number $n<Y$ has largest prime factor
\[
  \Pp(n)\;=\;q\;\le\;\Big\lfloor \tfrac{Y-1}{C}\Big\rfloor \;=\; 5518 .
\]
In particular every prime factor of such an $n$ lies among the $727$ odd primes $\le 5518$ (the
largest being $5507$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $q=\Pp(n)$ the largest factor; then $n=q\cdot(n/q)\ge q\,C$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:C}, and $n<Y$
gives $q\le(Y-1)/C$; evaluate the floor. Every other factor is smaller, hence also $\le 5518$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exhaustion}

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound]\label{thm:lower}
There is no Carmichael number $n<10^{145}$ with $\omega(n)=64$. Hence $\Smin\ge 10^{145}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (finite exhaustion)]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:univ} every candidate is a product of $64$ distinct primes drawn from the
universe $U$ of $727$ odd primes $\le 5518$, with product $<Y$. Enumerate all increasing
$64$--subsets of $U$ with product $<Y$, pruning a partial choice only by two \emph{necessary}
conditions: (i) fewer than the required number of primes remain; and (ii) the current product times
the $64-h$ smallest still-available primes individually compatible with the $h$ already chosen
already reaches $Y$ (an exact--integer $R_{\min}$ test). Pairwise admissibility
(Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}) is enforced during descent---once $p$ is chosen, every later prime $q$ with
$p\mid(q-1)$ is discarded---which removes no genuine Carmichael candidate, since any real completion
survives pairwise filtering and its remaining factors lie in the current available pool. At each
complete $64$--product, test Korselt.

The enumeration terminates with exactly $127092$ increasing pairwise--admissible $64$--subsets of
$U$ whose product is below $Y$ (equivalently $127092$ distinct products, by unique factorization),
of which \emph{none} is Carmichael. Therefore no $64$--factor Carmichael number is smaller than
$10^{145}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

The exhaustion was carried out by \emph{three separate enumeration implementations}, and the
universe bound $C$ was \emph{independently recomputed}; all agree. Everything below is at repository
revision \code{f872e32} of \href{https://github.com/AlexanderEastwood/carmichael-frontier}{\code{AlexanderEastwood/carmichael-frontier}}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \code{s64\_lower\_bound\_certificate.py} runs two enumerators: a binary include/exclude walk
with an incrementally maintained $\operatorname{lcm}(p_i-1)$ Korselt test ($327049$ reference
nodes), and an ordered--choice walk using per--prime divisibility, a separate prime generator, and
no bitmasks ($163525$ nodes). Both report $127092$ admissible products and $0$ Carmichael. They use
different prime generators, traversal logic, and Korselt predicates, while \emph{sharing} the
cofactor--bound computation and the results/hash reporting code; the identical order--sensitive leaf
digest \code{sha256 = e05f62598eea784b3f60f025c6ccbf30700115ecbef42702e24154dcece32321} is therefore
a reproducibility aid, not an additional independent correctness check.
\item \code{carmichael\_prime\_bound.py} independently recomputes $C$ and the cap $\Pp\le 5518$ from
first principles. It does \emph{not} enumerate products or establish the absence of Carmichael
numbers; it certifies only the universe.
\item \code{s64\_lower\_bound\_oracle\_check.py} is a separate clean--room enumerator (its own
prime generation and ordered traversal) that takes the Korselt verdict at every one of the $127092$
leaves from the frozen oracle \code{ref/ref\_carmichael.py::verify\_certificate} rather than from
its own arithmetic, again finding $0$ Carmichael. It was validated against the oracle's brute--force
\code{least\_with\_k} on small $k$: $k=3\!\to\!561$, $k=4\!\to\!41041$, $k=5\!\to\!825265$.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Exact commands: \code{python3 s64\_lower\_bound\_certificate.py};
\code{python3 carmichael\_prime\_bound.py -{}-X 1e145 -{}-pi};
\code{python3 s64\_lower\_bound\_oracle\_check.py}.

\section{The interval}

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} (lower) with \S\ref{sec:upper} (upper) proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:interval}: $10^{145}\le\Smin\le N<10^{148}$, so $\Smin$ has $146$, $147$, or $148$
digits, with remaining exclusion interval $[10^{145},N)$. This is a global statement about $\Smin$;
it is \emph{not} a determination of $\Smin$, which would require excluding every $64$--factor
Carmichael number in $[10^{145},N)$.

\begin{remark}[Strengthening to $147$--$148$ digits]
A complete exact--$k$ preproduct search over all $64$--factor products $\le 10^{146}$ returns no
Carmichael number, giving $10^{146}<\Smin\le N$, i.e.\ $\Smin$ has $147$ or $148$ digits. This is a
different tool from the enumerators of \S\ref{sec:repro} (a depth--first preproduct search with an
exact $R_{\min}$ bound and the $P\lambda(P)>B$ arithmetic--progression switch), and it is far cheaper
than the $\ge 3.89\times10^{10}$ admissible--product count below $10^{146}$ because it rejects
prefixes without materialising each product ($\approx 71$\,s, single core). \emph{Verification
tier}: the $\Smin\ge 10^{145}$ bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} is the triple--verified core; this
strengthening currently rests on the search engine, corroborated by two independent switch strategies
both returning ``none'' and by the same engine reproducing ``none'' at $10^{145}$ (a fourth
independent confirmation of that bound) and reproducing $S_{10}\dots S_{29}$ against
OEIS~A006931. An independent admissibility certificate at $10^{146}$ is the outstanding follow--up;
the full exclusion of $[10^{145},N)$ that would prove $\Smin=N$ remains a machine--scale endgame.
\end{remark}

\section{Transfer to $S_{65}$}\label{sec:s65}

The cofactor machinery transfers to $k=65$ with no new exhaustion. Let
\[
  A_{64}\;=\;\min\{\,\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{64}p_i : p_1<\dots<p_{64}\text{ odd primes, pairwise admissible}\,\}
\]
be the least product of $64$ pairwise--admissible odd primes. The enumeration of \S\ref{sec:lower}
records it as a by--product: it is the minimum over the $127092$ admissible $64$--products below
$10^{145}$ (that this global minimum lies below $10^{145}$ is witnessed by those products), namely the
explicit $145$--digit integer
\[
  A_{64}=4411723654458811690564281091321000614852933427803594841106730678799037685864777959408045502066896318764081869632059317133390275833282396201943377 .
\]

\begin{lemma}[Cofactor transfer]\label{lem:transfer}
For every Carmichael number $n$ with $\omega(n)=65$ and every prime $q\mid n$, the remaining $64$
factors form a pairwise--admissible $64$--product (Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}), so $n/q\ge A_{64}$. Hence any
such $n<U$ has $\Pp(n)\le\lfloor(U-1)/A_{64}\rfloor$.
\end{lemma}

Taking $U=10^{148}$ gives $\Pp\le 2266$: a universe of $334$ odd primes.

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound for $S_{65}$]\label{thm:s65}
There is no Carmichael number $n<10^{148}$ with $\omega(n)=65$. Hence $S_{65}\ge 10^{148}$, and the
smallest $65$--factor Carmichael number has at least $149$ digits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (finite exhaustion)]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} every candidate is a product of $65$ distinct primes from the $334$--prime
universe with product $<10^{148}$. An ordered--prefix search with exact product pruning descends until
$P\lambda(P)>10^{148}$ and then tests the at most one cofactor in the forced residue class
$R\equiv P^{-1}\pmod{\lambda(P)}$, enforcing $65$ distinct factors and full Korselt with primality
taken from the sieve. It visits $64355$ nodes, forms $66$ progression candidates, and finds no
Carmichael number.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reproduction and verification tier]
Theorem~\ref{thm:s65} was reproduced four ways, all agreeing on $0$ Carmichael numbers:
(i) the certificate \code{s65/s65\_exclusion.py} above; (ii) a separate native binary
enumeration \code{s65/verify65.cpp} that checks all $22{,}287{,}138$ admissible $65$--products below
$10^{148}$ without inverses or the progression shortcut; and (iii)--(iv) our preproduct search engine
in both switch modes (\S\ref{sec:repro}), which independently reports $n=$ none with matching
counts ($5012$ inversions, $66$ progression candidates). A companion certificate
\code{s65/s65\_transfer\_certificate.py} establishes the weaker $S_{65}\ge 3.17\times10^{147}$ by
extending the recorded $64$--products two independent ways ($285$ admissible $65$--products, $0$
Carmichael). All rest on the same $A_{64}$, whose defining $127092$--product set and digest are the
triple--verified core of \S\ref{sec:lower}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Interval for $S_{65}$]\label{thm:s65interval}
$10^{148}\le S_{65}\le U_{65}$, where $U_{65}$ is the $151$--digit Carmichael number with exactly $65$
prime factors $19,31,37,\dots,1021,1993$ (Webster's candidate). Consequently $S_{65}$ has $149$,
$150$, or $151$ decimal digits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:s65}. For the upper bound: by Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} every
$65$--factor Carmichael below $10^{151}$ has all prime factors $\le\lfloor(10^{151}-1)/A_{64}\rfloor=
2266687$, so $U_{65}$ (being $<10^{151}$) factors completely over that range. Independent trial
division confirms $U_{65}$ is a product of exactly $65$ distinct primes $\le 1993$; the frozen oracle
\code{ref/ref\_carmichael.py::verify\_certificate} confirms Korselt, so $U_{65}$ is Carmichael with
$\omega=65$ and $S_{65}\le U_{65}$. As $U_{65}$ has $151$ digits and $S_{65}\ge10^{148}$ has at least
$149$, the interval follows. (Factor list in \code{results\_k65\_webster\_verified.json}.)
\end{proof}
