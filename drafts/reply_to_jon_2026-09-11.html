Jon,

You're right, and the sentence was wrong. "We were deep at the wrong λ; you found the right one" was loose shorthand from my side of the conversation with the assistant, not a description of your algorithm, and it misrepresents it. Your search is an exhaustive search over preproducts; λ is a consequence of the preproduct, and your minimality guarantee comes from that exhaustion. I retract the characterisation. (The PDF itself does not describe your method; the only sentence about it says that your number "supplied a second modulus to investigate", which is all we can honestly say.)

On the substance, I agree with you, and the paper is careful to claim only what our computation shows:

  • What the exchange search does. Fix a modulus M. Every Carmichael number n with λ(n) = D has all its prime factors in Q(D) = {q : (q−1) | D, q ∤ D} and satisfies n ≡ 1 (mod D). For a given D and a bound U, the 64-subsets of Q(D) with product below U are finite and size-bounded (a cap on the largest prime and on the number of exchanges from the 64 smallest primes), so the meet-in-the-middle search at D is exhaustive for that D. Running it at every divisor D of M that can carry such a number gives a restricted minimum: no 64-factor Carmichael number below the bound has λ(n) | M.
  • What it does not do. It does not enumerate λ. Every candidate λ for n < U divides L = lcm{q−1 : q ≤ P+-bound}, but the divisors of L that could carry a 64-factor number are far too many to search, and we have no argument that any λ-first procedure is competitive with preproduct exhaustion. The two restricted minima in the paper (for λ(N₀) and for λ(N)) therefore say precisely this and no more: any 64-factor Carmichael number below N has λ(n) ∤ λ(N₀) and λ(n) ∤ λ(N). They do not locate S₆₄, and the paper says so (interval, restricted minima, "not a determination of S₆₄").
  • Where I think it is useful next to yours. As a fast certificate inside a family (minutes per modulus, records and an independent checker in the repository), as a positive control (with the bound frozen at the previous incumbent it rediscovers our N₀ at radius 8 and your N at radius 6 from the 64 smallest eligible primes), and as a cheap heuristic probe of nearby moduli — we ran one-step neighbours of both λ's (6,674 moduli, small exchange radii) and found nothing below N, which is a bounded negative, not evidence about S₆₄. Minimality for k = 64 rests on preproduct exhaustion, i.e. on your method.

Two things I'd value your view on, when you have time: whether you'd want the paper to state explicitly that the divisor-family approach is not an algorithm for S₆₄ and that minimality comes from preproduct exhaustion, with your method cited properly (I'm inclined to add exactly that sentence); and how far your exhaustion has progressed toward certifying S₆₄ — Alex to fill in / ask as appropriate.
